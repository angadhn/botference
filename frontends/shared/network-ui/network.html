What you are looking at

These are your projects, shown two ways; switch between them under View. The map shows each project as a dot in one of five rough groups (writing, teaching, research and ventures, health and wellbeing, other), guessed from its title and possibly wrong; a project in two groups sits between them, and being near each other means nothing more. A line means a connection between two projects was recorded: solid when you linked them or your writing uses one in the other, dashed when an agent only suggested it. A faint grey line only means both projects hold the same reading or piece of writing. Click a project to see its readings, writing and chats; the Back button returns you to where you were.

In the city each building is a project; its kind and the tint of its lawn are a first guess at its group, from the title. A building that says paused or archived, with its shutters closed, is one where work has stopped. A red flag means a dated update in the last 7 days. Point at a building to see how many of its readings were updated in the last 30 days, and to bring out its own roads. The windows, people, lamps and boats are decoration.

Roads follow recorded connections only: paved when linked or used in your writing, stepping stones when only suggested. Turn on Show routes under Filters to see them all. A linked road crosses water by a bridge and comes down a terrace by stairs; a suggested one never does. A cobbled square is where three or more linked projects meet. The ground is scenery chosen by what a building is for: a low terraced hill under observatories and lecture halls, an island under launch sites, woodland plain for the rest.

Connected projects are placed near each other. Drag empty ground to pan. Pinch or use the zoom buttons on a phone. To move a building on a phone, hold it still briefly, then drag; with a mouse, drag it directly. Drag it past the edge of the land and new ground is made for it there; the panel that opens then has Undo move. Positions and new land are saved in this browser only.
